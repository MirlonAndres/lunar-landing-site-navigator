1. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY & MISSION PURPOSE
================================================================================
The lunar south pole is humanity's strategic gateway for establishing a
sustainable, long-term presence on the Moon under NASA's Artemis program,
harboring extensive water-ice deposits trapped inside cryogenic Permanently
Shadowed Regions (PSRs). However, polar operations present two critical hazards:
  1. Extreme topography characterized by sharp crater rims, steep escarpments,
     and micro-scale surface roughness that risk lander tip-over upon touchdown.
  2. Extreme grazing solar angles (solar elevation hovering between 0.8° and
     1.5° above the horizon), causing flat, horizontal solar panels to capture
     virtually negligible sunlight (~26 W/m² due to sin(1.1°) ≈ 0.019).

The Lunar Landing Site Navigator solves both challenges within a unified,
mission-critical web teleoperation and planetary analysis platform:
  - Multi-Criteria Decision Analysis (MCDA) ingesting real NASA Lunar
    Reconnaissance Orbiter (LRO) datasets (LOLA altimetry, Diviner thermal,
    LEND epithermal hydrogen, and JPL Horizons celestial ephemerides).
  - Interactive 3D WebGL Digital Twin (Three.js) delivering seamless macro-to-
    micro navigation from the full Moon globe down to a 256x256 LOLA terrain mesh.
  - 5-DOF Articulated Robotic Arm with Solar Panel End-Effector that yields a
    50.1x energy harvest multiplier over conventional flat-mounted panels.
  - Real-Time Hardware-In-The-Loop (HIL) Teleoperation with an ESP32-S3 and
    PCA9685 I2C controller communicating over cloud WebSockets with sub-150 ms
    latency.
